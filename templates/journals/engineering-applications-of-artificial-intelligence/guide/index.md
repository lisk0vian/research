# Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence — Guide for Authors

Local, offline copy of the journal's Guide for Authors (ISSN 0952-1976, Elsevier
/ IFAC). The publisher's page sits behind a captcha, so this is the only copy
that survives. Provenance is in `SOURCES.md`; pages this guide links to but does
not contain are in `external.md`.

64 sections in 71 files, 1094 lines. Read the one that owns the question — the
whole guide costs more context than any single answer is worth.

**The sections are the guide verbatim**, cut on the publisher's own headings.
Nothing is summarised: these are the limits an editor screens on.

**Two things are not in the publisher's outline and are worth knowing about.**
EAAI desk-rejects a paper for any of four reasons — no metaphor-based
metaheuristics, an abstract that does not separate the AI contribution from the
engineering application, undefined acronyms in title or abstract, or a
single-column format violation — and they were buried in the middle of the scope
description, so they now have their own file. And the review model here is
**double anonymized**: the title page is a separate file from the manuscript.

## Notes

- **Bold entries** are the ones a submission is most often sent back for.
- **References is a folder**, not one 150-line file: `sections/51-references/`
  holds its own index and seven parts, because 150 lines is more context than
  any one citation question is worth.
- The publisher's grouping headings (*About the journal*, *Ethics and policies*,
  *Writing and formatting*, *Submitting your manuscript*, *After receiving a final
  decision*, *Resources for authors*, *Getting help and support*) are the headings
  above, not files: they carry no text of their own.
- The page's own table of contents is not reproduced. This index replaces it and,
  unlike it, resolves offline.
- Every link to a page outside the guide keeps its URL and is listed in
  `external.md` as `NOT MIRRORED`: the text is not in the bank and the URL is
  where to get it. None of those pages states a rule the guide does not already
  state.
- One link was rewritten: the article-withdrawal policy pointed at Elsevier's
  staging server, so it points at production. It is the only edit to the text.
