# Q1 pre-submission review — c19-2026 (re-scoped to Decision 0001)

- **Reviewer role:** Applied Geography referee (simulated, pre-submission)
- **Manuscript reviewed:** `paper/main.qmd` as it stands on 2026-10-30
- **Frame of review:** the contribution defined in `decision-0001-contribution.md`
  (district-level access to complex hospital care, travel-time based, with method
  sensitivity). This review judges the manuscript against that frame and against
  what the data currently allow.
- **Supersedes:** the chat review of 2026-10-30 (same date), which judged the
  classifier-based framing.

## Verdict

**Reject as submitted (desk rejection is likely).**

The current manuscript is not the paper described by Decision 0001. Its title, abstract,
highlights and main results are built on a classifier that the pipeline itself shows does
not generalize, on a target that is circular with its inputs, and on census joins that
were wrong until this week. None of the numbers in it can be defended as evidence for the
new question.

A resubmission is reasonable **only after** RQ1 has been answered with travel-time
access data at district level. The current data and text do not reach that bar, and
there is no reason to send an incomplete version now.

Two things a referee would still credit: the manuscript is candid about its own circularity
and leakage history, and the pipeline is reproducible (hashes, seeds, a versioned ETL cache).
Those strengths are worth keeping in the methods section of the new paper.

## Major issues

### M1. There is no answer to RQ1 yet (blocking)
- The only access measure is straight-line haversine distance to the nearest hospital of
  level ≥ II-1 (`_etl.py`, `NearestNeighbors(metric="haversine")`). Decision 0001 requires
  travel time or an E2SFCA-type measure.
- No road or river network is in the repository. Without it, the paper cannot claim that
  access is measured in time or cost.
- **What would fix it:** O2 resolved; access computed per district centroid (or per
  population cell), with the method stated and parameterised.

### M2. The validity test (RQ3) does not exist (blocking)
- The only "validation" is leave-region-out prediction of the INVS, which is circular
  (ρ = 0.92 for one input). It is not independent evidence.
- A referee will ask: *does the access index predict anything that is not built from the
  same RENIPRESS and census inputs?* Right now the answer is no.
- **What would fix it:** O1 resolved, with a stated outcome, its source, and a pre-declared
  test. Without an independent outcome the paper falls back to contribution B, which has a
  weaker venue fit.

### M3. The unit of analysis is misreported
- The manuscript says n = 26,638 facilities. The target and every predictor are constant
  within each district (0 of 1,889 districts vary), so the effective sample for any
  district-level claim is 1,889 units, not 26,638.
- Access is a property of places and populations. The paper must be explicit that facilities
  are inputs and districts (or population cells) are the units of inference.
- **What would fix it:** state the unit in §2; report all counts at the unit of inference.

### M4. The census join is corrected, but the sample is not final (must report)
- After the fix (`_etl.py`), 16 districts created after 2017 remain without population
  (38 active facilities), and they are excluded. This is a sample decision, not a detail.
- The manuscript must report: 1,874 INEI districts used; 16 post-2017 districts excluded;
  the facility count before and after; and a comparison of excluded and included districts
  on access. A referee will look for selection bias here.
- The earlier numbers (26,638 facilities; 1,889 districts; 6.19 % prevalence) are not valid
  and must be re-derived from the corrected run.

### M5. Regional equity relies on a coarse, partly wrong mapping
- Region is assigned per department. Arequipa, Moquegua and Tacna are "Costa"; Huánuco is
  "Selva"; anything unmapped defaults to "Sierra" (`fillna("Sierra")`).
- Because the old "Sierra inequity" finding depends on this mapping, it must not be
  reported. The new paper needs district-level regions (O5) before any equity statement.

### M6. Literature for the chosen method is absent
- The introduction and methods do not cite the accessibility literature the new question
  depends on: two-step floating catchment and enhanced variants (Luo & Wang, 2003; Luo & Qi,
  2009), and global travel-time and friction-surface work (Weiss et al., 2020). Delamater
  (2013) on spatial accessibility concepts is also expected.
- These references must be verified with `paper-search` before use (O6). Citations from
  memory are not accepted, and no citation in this list has been verified yet.

### M7. The paper has no sensitivity structure yet
- RQ2 is the paper's second main result, and nothing in the current pipeline varies the
  method. A referee will expect a table of rankings under alternative choices (metric,
  catchment size, decay, facility level), with the stability of the top-k list reported.
- **What would fix it:** a parameter grid and a rank-stability metric (e.g. top-k overlap,
  Kendall's tau), reported in one table.

## Carried over from the earlier review (still valid, re-scoped)

These were raised on the classifier-based manuscript and still apply where the same
component survives in the new paper.

- **Spatial statistics (old M4), still open.** Moran's I was computed on a 1,500-facility
  random sample, with a distance threshold of 1.5 in unprojected degrees (~165 km). Getis
  p-values are identical to LISA p-values in 100 % of rows, so they are not independent
  evidence. Rebuild on district polygons, with all units, a projected CRS, and FDR control.
- **Missing coordinates (old M5), still open.** 37 % of facilities lack coordinates; their
  distances are imputed from the district median. The new paper must test whether
  unlocated facilities are selected by region or by facility level.
- **Temporal mismatch (old M7), still open.** RENIPRESS is current; the census is 2017.
  The paper must justify the mix or use INEI projections.
- **Calibration (old M2).** Irrelevant if the classifier leaves the paper. If any model
  remains, ECE 0.17 and Hosmer–Lemeshow p ≈ 0 disqualify it as a predictor of need.
- **Optimisation benefit (old M3).** Optuna changed AUC by about 0.01; no claim should
  depend on it.
- **Cost–benefit analysis (old reviewer note).** Current ratio 0.06 is built on the
  classifier's probabilities. Defer it (Decision 0001, §5).

## What would make this acceptable (acceptance criteria)

The paper is ready for a first submission when all of the following hold:

1. RQ1, RQ2 and RQ3 are answered with the data described in the decision record, and each
   claim C-A, C-B, C-C is either supported or reported as not supported.
2. Every number in the manuscript comes from a file in `outputs/` produced by the corrected
   pipeline (the `paper-validate` and gate-claims checks pass).
3. Methods state the unit of inference, the sample (including the 16 excluded districts),
   the access model and its parameters, and the sensitivity grid.
4. The introduction and methods cite the accessibility literature, verified with
   `paper-search`.
5. The manuscript complies with Applied Geography's Guide for Authors, checked with
   `paper-compliance`: abstract and highlight limits, review model, and reference style.
6. The independent outcome (RQ3) is named in the title or abstract, so a reader can judge
   the validity claim without opening the paper.
