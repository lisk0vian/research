# Elsevier tutorial: what to include in a cover letter

Source: Elsevier Researcher Academy video "What to include in the cover
letter" (Journal Article Publishing Support Center), transcribed 2026-10-10.

## Key points of the tutorial

- The cover letter accompanies the submission and gives author information,
  the key findings and significance of the study, information on additional
  data and supplementary material, and on ethical compliance.
- It is mandatory for some journals and optional for others: the journal's
  Guide for Authors says which (Editorial Manager -> About -> Instructions for
  Authors, or the journal home page on ScienceDirect).
- Purpose: show that the manuscript reflects authentic research, and show the
  editor why it fits the journal, like a cover letter for a job application.
- Keep it **short and focused**. Start with the aim of the study and the main
  findings; avoid unnecessary detail; focus on what makes the research
  important.
- Explain how the study fits the journal's **aims and scope**.
- Highlight the **novelty** of the research, its broader implications and its
  potential impact on the field.
- If the manuscript was **invited**, say so.
- Do **not** include funding information, author declarations, or suggested or
  opposed reviewers, unless the journal specifically requests them.
- Journals may set their own content and format requirements: read the Guide
  for Authors first.
- A submitted manuscript is considered final: upload every file, the cover
  letter included, before submitting, and check that the letter represents the
  study accurately and follows the journal's guidelines.

## If the Guide for Authors does not specify the content

You may wish to include some of the following:

- Special considerations that should be given to the paper (if any).
- A brief background regarding the research involved or how the data were
  collected.
- Details of any previous or concurrent submissions.
- Any information that will support the submission (e.g. original or
  confirmatory data, relevance, topicality).

## Template shown in the tutorial

```
[Your Name]
[Your Organization/Institutional Affiliation]

[Editor's name if known]
[Journal Name]
[Journal Address]

[Date of Submission]

Dear [Editor name],
We wish to submit an original research paper entitled "[title of article]"
to be published by [name of the journal].

This paper addresses the problem of [main question addressed by study].
Studies have shown that [insert brief background of study]. However, it needs
to be clarified whether [insert problem statement]. Using [insert method], we
show that [describe key finding(s) of the study]. Our findings are significant
because [explain the impact of the study].

Since our study deals with [explain how the main focus of the study aligns
with the journal's scope], we believe it may be a good fit for publication in
[name of journal]. We feel that the findings of our study will be highly
relevant to your audience since [explain the research contribution of study
or practical implications].

The article "[title of article]" has not been published elsewhere, and it
reflects original research conducted by its authors. None of the authors have
any conflicts of interest to disclose concerning this study.

Thank you for your valuable time.

Sincerely,

[Your name]
[Your title]
[Your email address]
```

`scripts/paper_cover_letter.py --init` reproduces this layout. For an
online-only journal the "Journal Address" line is the publisher (Elsevier);
use a postal address only when the journal's site gives one for the
editorial office.
